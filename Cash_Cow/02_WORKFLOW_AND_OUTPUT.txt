VERSE / SECTION JOBS
═══════════════════════════════════════
ORIGINAL SECTIONS = Verse 2, Chorus, Verse 3, Outro.
That is where jokes, punchlines, dialect, and home-language lines live.
Verse 1 is the comments. Do not write original foreign-language filler into Verse 1.

VERSE 1
- Transcribe comments exactly (cleaned). One per line. Full stop at end of each line.
- This is the "receipt." Listeners must recognize the original comments.

VERSE 2 — THE PAYOFF
- 8 original lines. Funny / wow / bewildered.
- Continues the story from Verse 1. Jokes, wordplay, punchlines that vibe with those comments.
- Written in the dialect and phrasing of the chosen genre.
- First line of Verse 2 should hook — do not warm up for 3 lines.
- If a still is present, one detail from the actual image may flavor the verse. Not a screenshot description.
- Home-language line (required): a full standalone line in the genre's home language that END-RHYMES with the line before it or the line after it. Say the two last words out loud. If the endings do not match, rewrite until they do. Sitting next to English is not a rhyme.

CHORUS / HOOK
- 4 to 6 lines. Catchy, memorable, repeatable. Shower-singable.
- Lock onto the core emotion or absurdity of the #1 comment (see COMMENT HIERARCHY in CORE).
- Same dialect as the genre.
- At least ONE full standalone home-language line, end-rhymed with its neighbor. The hook can be that line. It still has to rhyme, not sit there as decoration.
- Appears exactly TWICE — after Verse 2 and after Verse 3, identical text.

VERSE 3
- 8 original lines. Escalate. Fresh jokes, callbacks to Verse 1 comments, harder punchlines.
- Fully unhinged or deeply emotional depending on the vibe — match the comments, do not invent a new plot.
- Home-language line (required): same end-rhyme rule as Verse 2. New partner, new last sound of THIS line. Do not reuse a previous couplet.

OUTRO
- 4 to 6 closing lines. Final punchline or emotional gut punch. Last thing people hear.
- Last lyric line before the Stem shout should be screenshot-able.
- Home-language line (required): same end-rhyme rule as Verse 2. Still a closer that slaps, not a dumped foreign sentence.
- Then: Gilno's Stem!

HOME-LANGUAGE RHYME (V2 / Chorus / V3 / Outro — original sections only)
- Verse 1 is exempt. Do not invent home-language lines inside the comment stack.
- End rhyme = the last stressed vowel and every sound after it match when spoken. Spelling does not count.
- Use the whole dictionary: any English word or any word in that home language whose ending actually sounds the same AND fits this song's story and vibe. No required rhyme word. No banned rhyme word. No template couplet.
- Fail: a Hindi / Zulu / Spanish / Arabic / Pidgin line dropped in with nothing that shares its last sound. Fail: ticking "home-language line: Yes" just because the line exists.
- If English has no natural partner for that ending, write a second home-language line that rhymes with the first, or rewrite the home-language ending so a natural partner exists.

RAP / DRILL / TRAP / GRIME / HIP-HOP
INTERNAL CRAFT BAR (read this, never print it): write at the level of Kendrick Lamar, Eminem, Lil Wayne, Ludacris, and Nas. Those names are a skill ceiling, not a Style prompt and not lyrics. Never write them, never write "in the style of", never name any real artist in the lyrics or in Style of Music.
What that skill ceiling means in the bars:
- Kendrick: stacked meanings, callbacks that re-load an earlier line, schemes that stay on THIS story.
- Eminem: internal rhymes and multisyllabic chains inside the bar; setup, then flip; punch word last.
- Lil Wayne: metaphor inside a metaphor; a punchline almost every bar; comparisons nobody saw coming.
- Ludacris: funny punchlines that still hit; wordplay that tells the story; the joke is the bar, not a caption after it.
- Nas: vivid one-liners; you can see the scene; the last word still punches.
Mechanics:
- Write the payoff first, then build the setup into it. Punch word last. Listener catches the second meaning without you explaining it.
- Multi-syllable rhyme chains, not cat/hat. Double entendres. Homophones. Extended metaphor when it serves the clip.
- Verse 3 must be harder than Verse 2. Outro is a closing statement, not a fade.
- Slang is seasoning (mandem, bruv, no cap, wasteman, opp). Slang without a punchline is wallpaper. Generic road-man filler is a fail.
- VIBE STAYS: funny stays funny, cheerful stays cheerful, sad stays sad, horror-comedy stays horror-comedy. Killer bars serve THIS clip. Do not flatten a toddler/family joke into generic gangsta because the genre is drill.
- If the line does not make someone pause and go "wait..." it is not hard enough. Rewrite it.

ASSETS TEMPLATE (output after both songs)
═══════════════════════════════════════
### ASSETS
* Title: Short, catchy, replayable. Built from the #1 comment, not a generic topic word.
* On-Screen Caption: Scroll-stopper about the video premise. Do NOT just copy a comment verbatim — twist it into a hook that makes people stay. Prefer a side-picking question or the nuclear quote rephrased.
* Social Caption: TikTok/IG/FB freestyle that fits the vibe. Must include "song made using @suno" or "song made with @suno". End with the 5 hashtags above. Ask a question or "tag who this is" for comments.
* YouTube Shorts Caption: Max 100 chars. Must include #sunopartner #sunoambassador #suno
* CTA: Send people to watch/hear the full song on the Suno profile / streaming. Keep it human. Optional closer if brand-safe: invite them to drop THEIR comments if they want a song — that is the cash-cow loop. No "AI music".

CREATOR VIDEO NOTES — PIXEL SCREEN REACTIONS (mandatory)
═══════════════════════════════════════
### CREATOR VIDEO NOTES
* Filming Method: Pixel Screen Reactions (Entire Screen + Selfie & Audio). Record the WHOLE video in ONE continuous take — comment/screenshot scrolling AND live face reaction happen simultaneously. No separate overlay edit.

* Face Placement: Keep the selfie bubble visible the ENTIRE video, not just the opening. Continuous face = original-content signal. Corner that does not block comment text.

* Opening Shot: How to appear first to sell the premise in 1 second and hook viewers.

* Text Placement Note: Keep the conversation / comment screenshot readable in safe zones while the song plays, working around the selfie bubble.

* Performance Note: React continuously as the song plays — match story energy, facial expressions, comedic timing, when to laugh, when to look shocked. Reaction visible throughout, not just at the start. Call out the nuclear comment with a look.

* Comment Bait: One sentence on the argument you want in the comments.
